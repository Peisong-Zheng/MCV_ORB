## Thirumalai et al. (2020): the central introduction connection

**Key:** `thirumalai2020methane`. Thirumalai, K., Clemens, S. C., and Partin, J. W., *Methane, Monsoons, and Modulation of Millennial-Scale Climate*, GRL 47(9), e2020GL087613. [Publisher](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2020GL087613).

The complete 11-page PDF supplied by the author was read. Page references below are the article's printed pages, which also match the PDF pages. Extracted text is temporary at `/private/tmp/thirumalai2020.txt`.

- **What they measured (pp. 2–3):** the magnitude of millennial variability (MMV), calculated as a centered 2-kyr rolling standard deviation of a 10-kyr high-pass-filtered signal, with a 100-year step. They also examined positive and negative amplitude envelopes and their spectra. The principal comparison covers 640 kyr of EPICA methane and composite Chinese speleothem oxygen isotopes. This is not a likelihood model of an onset catalogue.
- **What they found (pp. 4–6; Figs. 1–4):** strong precession-band modulation of methane MMV, with larger variability near precession minima / Northern Hemisphere summer insolation maxima; no corresponding precession-band modulation of Chinese speleothem MMV. The latter resembles Antarctic isotope MMV. An obliquity-band spectral feature in the Chinese MMV does not give a persistent in-phase relationship to orbital forcing.
- **Their interpretation (pp. 7–9):** methane-producing environments in northern middle/high latitudes could respond to seasonal insolation, whereas internal ice–ocean–atmosphere feedbacks could account for the Chinese/Antarctic amplitude correspondence. This is an interpretation, conditional on the proxy interpretation. They explicitly retain uncertainty over tropical versus boreal methane sources.
- **Barker connection (p. 8):** they discuss the similar MMV of Antarctic isotopes and Barker's synthetic Greenland series and the assumptions of a stationary bipolar-seesaw reconstruction. Our positive event-rate association for the SpeleoAge catalogue is not a direct replication or rejection of their amplitude analysis.
- **How to use it:** introduce it alongside, rather than subordinate it to, positive orbital-modulation findings. It demonstrates that the apparent role of orbital forcing depends on the record and statistic. The proposed gap is whether precession contributes to a conditional warming-onset rate after accounting for climate background and event history.
- **Avoid:** “Thirumalai showed that speleothems are unrelated to precession”; their raw Chinese isotope series has strong precession variance. Also avoid claiming our present catalogue overturns their result: proxies, periods and estimands differ. Their final paragraphs use broader amplitude/frequency language; identify the actual rolling-variance analysis when describing the evidence.

Suggested connection, to paraphrase in the manuscript: *Evidence for orbital modulation is proxy dependent: methane's millennial variability shows precession modulation that is absent from the amplitude envelope of the Chinese speleothem composite. Such contrasts motivate separating the magnitude of fluctuations from the conditional timing of individual warming onsets.*

## Narrative guardrails

1. Lead with the literature's mixed evidence: orbital/background modulation of variability, Thirumalai's proxy contrast, and prior stochastic/state-duration analyses. Then state the narrower present question.
2. Introduce “conditional association” as an estimand: improvement in the fitted warming-onset rate given selected background/history terms. This supports neither deterministic pacing nor uniqueness of an insolation pathway.
3. In the discussion, a shared role for seasonal insolation is a compatible physical reading, not an inference that these cited model mechanisms produced our events.
4. Preserve the distinction between amplitude modulation, event occurrence per total observed time, and cold-state transition hazard. The current results cannot be substituted for the other two measures.
5. Background adjustment may absorb indirect orbital effects. Weak incremental eccentricity/obliquity information therefore does not exclude those mechanisms. Correlation between precession phase and summer insolation also limits attribution.

## Greenland model-extension chronology: checked wording

- **Southon (2004), printed pp. 1244 and 1246:** the original ss09 model used a younger layer-counted constraint and an older MIS 5d constraint at 110 ka taken from the Martinson et al. (1987) SPECMAP chronology. Its successor ss09sea retained a two-tie-point glaciological framework, with revised accumulation and younger counting constraints. [Publisher DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033822200033129). The publisher PDF was downloaded to temporary storage and these sections read directly.
- **Wolff et al. (2010), printed pp. 2830–2831:** the GICC05 extension beyond 60 ka adopts ss09sea with a 705-year younger shift to match the counted chronology. The local published PDF was read directly. [Publisher DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.quascirev.2009.10.013).
- **Supported manuscript statement:** the modeled Greenland extension inherits a chronology framework whose development used a marine-isotope-stage age constraint derived from SPECMAP. This is a dependence of chronology construction; it is not evidence that individual NGRIP warmings were aligned to precession. Avoid describing the complete NGRIP record, especially its layer-counted portion, as orbitally tuned.
