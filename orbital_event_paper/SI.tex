\section*{Text S1. Event selection, timing, and observation intervals}

The speleothem composite retains MF labels 6.1--6.16 and adds Sofular labels 6.9--6.13 as composite events 6.17--6.21 (Figure~\ref{fig:S02}; Table~\ref{tab:S1}). In the overlap, MF 6.16 corresponds to an unnumbered Sofular feature near 175.2 kyr BP, which is therefore not added as a separate event; Sofular 6.9 is the next older distinct transition (Figure~\ref{fig:S01}). The older continuation uses Sofular, so the 7.08-kyr gap in the MF proxy does not interrupt the composite observation interval (Figure~\ref{fig:S01}).

Speleothem DO-like event times are assigned from the archived proxy series \citep{fohlmeister2023mfData,held2024sofularData}. Each series is divided at gaps exceeding 0.5 kyr, interpolated onto a 1-yr grid within each continuous segment, and Gaussian-smoothed with a 50-yr standard deviation. We select the largest gradient in the expected transition direction within $\pm400$ yr of each published label's approximate age: decreasing MF $\delta^{18}$O or increasing Sofular $\delta^{13}$C toward older BP ages. Search windows stop at data gaps and the midpoints between adjacent label ages. Table~\ref{tab:S1} lists the resulting event times, shown on the proxy records in Figure~\ref{fig:S02}. Nine timing alternatives combine smoothing scales of 35, 50, and 75 yr with search half-windows of 300, 400, and 500 yr; these change assigned times while retaining the same events.

The nominal observation windows are 12--123 kyr BP for the undisturbed NGRIP record \citep{ngrip2004high} and 132.5--204.5 kyr BP for the speleothem composite \citep{held2024dansgaard}. Starting at the oldest event in each sequence restricts the fitted intervals to 12--115.320 and 132.5--194.238 kyr BP, respectively, giving 165.058 kyr of observation time. No time from the gap between the records enters the likelihood. The boundaries reflect source coverage; the analysis assumes adequate event representation within them, rather than a demonstrably complete inventory. Descriptive event-phase plots retain all 55 events (Figure~2), including the two used only to initialize history in the rate model.

Barker event identities and ages are taken directly from the warming-event columns of Supplementary Table~S3 in \citet{barker2011800}, using SpeleoAge and retaining their published threshold classifications. Applying the same initialization rule to the variable- and fixed-threshold definitions leaves 69 fitted events over 0--396.464 kyr BP and 58 over 0--392.246 kyr BP, respectively. Their different warming starting events and interval lengths preclude a direct likelihood ranking; the two definitions test the same reconstruction rather than providing separate replications.

\section*{Text S2. Event-definition and chronology uncertainty}

We consider two sources of uncertainty in event ages. Event-definition uncertainty concerns where the onset is placed within a recorded transition, whereas chronology uncertainty concerns the age assigned to that part of the record. NGRIP realizations combine published definition-error categories with correlated chronology shifts. MF and Sofular realizations combine alternative onset-picking settings with age-model perturbations. For Barker, chronology is perturbed while event-definition sensitivity is tested separately using the two published threshold catalogues. Each main ensemble contains 10,000 realizations and preserves the published event identities and their order; sensitivity to missing events is considered separately in Text~S8. All ages are expressed in kyr before 1950 CE (kyr BP), with source chronologies converted to this common reference where necessary.

\subsection*{NGRIP}

We jointly perturb 34 warming and 35 cooling boundaries so that their alternating sequence remains ordered, then retain the warming ages for the event-rate analysis. Definition uncertainties follow Table~2 of \citet{rasmussen2014stratigraphic} and range from a few years to about 200 yr. We draw independent, zero-mean Gaussian offsets with standard deviations set by these uncertainties (Figure~\ref{fig:S03}a).

Within the 12--123-kyr BP study window, the chronology is annually layer-counted from 12 to 60.152 kyr BP and model-extended from 60.152 to 123 kyr BP. The annual GICC05 maximum counting error (MCE) increases to 2.611 kyr at the counting endpoint; we use MCE/2 as the chronology standard deviation \citep{svensson2008sixty,rasmussen2022gicc05}. Uncertainty in GICC05modelext is not quantified in the published chronology \citep{corrick2020synchronous}. Using the approximately 4.5\% uncertainty at approximately 120 kyr BP obtained by \citet{moseley2020nalps19} through extrapolation of the counted-age uncertainty trend, we adopt a constant relative envelope of $\pm4.5\%$ for the modeled section and interpret it approximately as $2\sigma$. Applying this percentage throughout the extension is a simplification of their extrapolation.

To generate a shared chronology shift rather than independent errors at every event, we place computational control ages on a 5-kyr grid from 0 to 120 ka b2k. This grid spans all retained boundaries. We replace the 60-ka grid point with the exact counting endpoint at 60.202 ka; the next point is 65 ka. Each grid point receives the chronology standard deviation described above, with zero uncertainty at age zero. For points ordered toward older ages, let $V_j$ be the squared standard deviation and set $V_{-1}=0$. Draw independent increments and accumulate them as
\begin{equation}
 e_j\sim N(0,V_j-V_{j-1}),\qquad \delta_j=\sum_{m\leq j}e_m.
\end{equation}
Thus each older control inherits the younger controls' accumulated shift and an additional random increment. Its marginal variance is $V_j$ before order constraints. The increment standard deviation is $\sqrt{V_j-V_{j-1}}$.

An event's chronology shift is interpolated linearly between the surrounding grid-point shifts, including across the counted--modeled boundary. Its realized age is the nominal age plus this shared chronology shift and its independent definition offset. We reject the entire proposal if the shifted grid ages or the combined warming/cooling boundaries change order, and repeat until 10,000 sequences are accepted (Figure~\ref{fig:S03}b). The stated envelopes set Gaussian sampling scales rather than hard limits on possible offsets. Text~S6 compares alternative grid spacings.

\subsection*{MF and Sofular}

Event-definition uncertainty is represented by the nine smoothing/search-window combinations described in Text~S1. Table~\ref{tab:S1} lists the minimum and maximum onset shifts relative to the default settings for all 16 MF and five Sofular events. Most timing ranges are no wider than 40 yr; MF events 6.10 and 6.14 span 211 and 365 yr, respectively. Each realization selects one combination with equal probability for all MF events and, independently, one for all Sofular events. We then apply the chronology shifts below, without adding separate picking noise or changing event identities.

For MF, \citet{fohlmeister2023role} provide nominal ages and lower and upper age bounds with the archived stack \citep{fohlmeister2023mfData}. We interpret these as an approximate 95\% uncertainty envelope and interpolate the bounds within continuous proxy segments. At each assigned onset, we take the larger distance from its assigned age to either bound and divide by 1.96 to obtain a Gaussian standard deviation. Each realization draws one standard-normal value and multiplies it by the local standard deviation at every MF event. This gives shared chronology shifts with the same sign and locally varying magnitudes, producing the strong within-record correlations in Figure~\ref{fig:S04}b.

The main Sofular ensemble uses ten So-4 U--Th controls and the published age--depth curve \citep{held2024dansgaard,held2024sofularData}. We perturb the reference-curve age at each dated depth with an independent, zero-mean Gaussian offset whose standard deviation is half the reported $2\sigma$ dating error. Each stack event is projected to its age-equivalent depth on So-4; neighboring control offsets are then linearly interpolated along the reference age coordinate and added to the event age. This preserves the published nominal chronology and growth-curve shape between controls. Perturbed controls must remain ordered; interpolation neither crosses the growth hiatus nor extends beyond dated/proxy coverage.

After MF and Sofular shifts are combined, proposals that reverse the composite event order are rejected; Figure~\ref{fig:S04}a shows the resulting event-age ranges. An alternative ensemble uses So-57 for source events 6.9--6.12 and retains So-4 for event 6.13, which lies outside So-57 coverage. Shifting the projection ages by up to $\pm1$ kyr tests the assumed transfer location without changing nominal event ages. The age-equivalent projection does not identify physically matched event layers. It transfers dating errors onto published curves rather than rerunning StalAge or iscam \citep{scholz2011stalage,fohlmeister2012statistical}; additional growth-history, systematic dating, stack-alignment, proxy-measurement, and inter-record climate-lag uncertainties are not simulated.

\subsection*{Barker}

The chronology ensemble uses the 70 variable-threshold events. Supplementary Table~S1 of \citet{barker2011800} provides 60 controls linking the synthetic record to the speleothem chronology, with combined matching and absolute-dating uncertainties (Figure~\ref{fig:S05}a). The source does not specify a probability distribution for these combined values. We use each as a uniform proposal half-width $U_j$, drawing independent shifts $\delta_j\sim\mathrm{Uniform}(-U_j,U_j)$. The components are not added again. For a nominal event age $t$ between controls of SpeleoAge $a_j$ and $a_{j+1}$, the event shift is
\begin{equation}
 \delta(t)=(1-w)\delta_j+w\delta_{j+1},\qquad
 w=\frac{t-a_j}{a_{j+1}-a_j},
\end{equation}
and its realized age is $t+\delta(t)$. Neighboring events share control shifts, giving correlated event-age errors. All interpolation uses SpeleoAge.

Seven events lie in the alignment gap bracketed by controls at 264.24 and 317.70 kyr; their shifts use the same interpolation. Four events are older than the oldest published control at 378.15 kyr. We add an auxiliary control at 400 kyr, with a 1.30-kyr half-width obtained by taking the larger of a linear extrapolation through the last four published uncertainties and their maximum. Control ages must remain ordered, which also preserves the order of interpolated events. We retain 10,000 accepted sequences (Figure~\ref{fig:S05}b).

These combined control uncertainties describe chronological alignment and dating, not an independent error for locating every warming onset. We therefore add no separate event-picking jitter to the Barker chronology ensemble. Event-definition sensitivity is assessed by refitting the 59-event fixed-threshold catalogue at its nominal ages and comparing its phase response with the variable-threshold result (Main Figure~2; Text~S6). This definition comparison is separate from the chronology Monte Carlo analysis.

\section*{Text S3. Continuous conditional likelihood and null calibration}

For each segment $r$, let $a$ denote age in kyr BP and $a_{r0}$ the age of its oldest observed event, on which we condition. Elapsed time $u=a_{r0}-a$ advances from zero at that event to $T_r$ at the younger observation boundary. Thus, an earlier event has $a_{rj}>a$, equivalently $u_{rj}<u$. We write
\begin{align}
 H_r(u)&=\sum_{j:0\leq u_{rj}<u}\exp[-(u-u_{rj})/\tau],\qquad \tau=1.5\ {\rm kyr},\\
 \eta_r^{\rm BG}(u)&=\beta_0+\beta_H H_r(u)+\beta_L L_r(u)+\beta_C C_r(u)+\beta_S S_r,\\
 \eta_r^{\rm full}(u)&=\eta_r^{\rm BG}(u)+\beta_s\sin\phi_r(u)+\beta_c\cos\phi_r(u),\qquad \lambda_r(u)=\exp[\eta_r(u)].
\end{align}
Barker omits the segment contrast $S_r$. This conditional-intensity construction allows history and external covariates to enter the same event model \citep{truccolo2005point,kass2014analysis}. Event-history kernels also appear in the self-exciting models of \citet{hawkes1971spectra}; here the kernel enters the log intensity and represents inhibition.

History includes the conditioning event, excludes the event occurring at the evaluation time, and remains separate across the record gap. The kernel has no finite cutoff. Unobserved pre-anchor history is zero in the main analysis. The constraint $\beta_H\leq0$ represents inhibition after recent events. On finite response intervals it also bounds the intensity by its exogenous background, avoiding positive-feedback escalation. The decay time, sign constraint and initialization are working assumptions; Text~S6 varies decay and initialization.

The conditional log likelihood \citep[Chapter~7]{daley2003introduction} is
\begin{equation}
 \ell=\sum_r\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{N_r}\eta_r(u_{ri})-\int_0^{T_r}\exp[\eta_r(u)]\,du\right\}.
\end{equation}
Event intensities use left-limit history. The integral includes event-free time after the youngest event; neither the anchor nor older exposure enters the likelihood. Linear interpolants of LR04 and \ce{CO2} are centered by nominal exposure-time means and divided by their ranges over that exposure. Their scales remain fixed in chronology and specification comparisons. La2004 precession minima and maxima define phase 0 and $\pi$, with unwrapped phase increasing linearly toward older BP ages between extrema \citep{laskar2004long}. The same interpolant supplies phases at event and integration times.

Positive Gauss--Legendre quadrature evaluates the intensity integral between actual events, external interpolation knots and phase extrema. Four interior nodes per interval give the working rule; higher-order integration is checked as numerical accuracy rather than treated as a scientific sensitivity axis. The quadrature-node count does not define statistical sample size. Coefficients maximize this objective subject to the history bound; numerical failures retain their identities and are resolved on the same event sequences.

For $A=(\beta_s^2+\beta_c^2)^{1/2}$,
\begin{equation}
 \phi_{\max}=\operatorname{atan2}(\beta_s,\beta_c)\pmod{2\pi},\qquad R_{\max/\min}=\exp(2A).
\end{equation}
The phase multiplier is $\exp(\beta_s\sin\phi+\beta_c\cos\phi)$ at fixed background and history; phase is unidentified at $A=0$. Rayleigh statistics describe unconditional inventory-phase concentration separately \citep{mardia2000directional}. With $N=\sum_rN_r$, $G=(\ell_{\rm full}-\ell_{\rm BG})/(N\ln2)$ measures in-sample gain in bits/event. $LR=2(\ell_{\rm full}-\ell_{\rm BG})$ uses a nominal $\chi^2_2$ reference for the added phase block. Positive $G$ alone is insufficient evidence. AIC is $-2\ell+2k$ for $k$ fitted coefficients, giving full-minus-BG AIC $4-LR$ on identical support. Its penalty is approximate when a parameter lies at the history constraint.

For each main point-age comparison, 9,999 catalogues are simulated from the fitted BG model \citep{davison1997bootstrap}. We simulate continuous event times by thinning \citep{ogata1981simulation}, proceeding from old to young under piecewise upper bounds on the exogenous rate; accepted events update inhibitory history immediately. Exact conditioning events, response boundaries and forcing interpolants are fixed. Both models, including nuisance coefficients, are refitted; response totals vary. With $B$ replicates,
\begin{equation}
 p_{\rm boot}=\frac{1+\sum_{b=1}^{B}\mathbf1(LR_b^*\geq LR_{\rm obs})}{B+1}.
\end{equation}
There are 2 and 76 exceedances among 9,999 primary and Barker simulations, giving $p=0.0003$ and $0.0077$ (Figure~\ref{fig:bootstrap_comparison}). The respective 95\% binomial intervals for null exceedance probability are 0.000024--0.000722 and 0.00599--0.00950; these quantify Monte Carlo precision, not effect uncertainty. All replicates enter the final calibration. Alternative specifications use their stated nominal references.

\section*{Text S4. Chronology sensitivity of the results}

Independent permutations pair 10,000 NGRIP and speleothem realizations without replacement. Each draw retains its event identities and exact ages, and rebuilds event history and the response interval after its own oldest conditioning event. Nominal forcing interpolants, climate scaling and observation boundaries remain fixed. Draws outside observation support are recorded without fitting, rather than clipped or resampled.

There are 9,982 valid primary fits and 18 unsupported draws. Valid fits retain 53 response events over 153.837--172.970 kyr. Median $G$ is 0.225 bits/event (2.5th--97.5th percentiles: 0.124--0.291), preferred phase $328.4^\circ$ ($311.6$--$342.8^\circ$), and maximum/minimum rate ratio 6.44 (3.72--9.20). Nominal $p<0.05$ in 9,961 fits, or 99.79\% of valid draws (Figure~\ref{fig:S06}).

All 10,000 Barker draws are supported, with 69 response events over 395.213--397.716 kyr. Median $G$ is 0.099 bits/event (0.071--0.131), preferred phase $336.9^\circ$ ($328.2$--$347.1^\circ$), and rate ratio 3.07 (2.55--3.72). Nominal $p<0.05$ in 9,948 draws (99.48\%; Figure~\ref{fig:S07}). These ranges describe sensitivity to the adopted chronology construction; they are not process-sampling confidence intervals, and threshold fractions are not empirical null p values.

\section*{Text S5. Effect precision under full-model simulations}

Three analyses distinguish chronology sensitivity from event-process sampling uncertainty. A uses the 9,982 chronology refits. B simulates 5,000 catalogues from the nominal-age full model. C selects 200 distinct valid chronology fits uniformly without replacement and simulates 50 catalogues from each, giving 10,000 refits. Simulations update history sequentially, allow response totals to vary and refit all coefficients. Each generator retains its own exact oldest events, response support and nominal climate scaling. In C, chronology enters through the fitted generator; simulated events receive no second age perturbation.

For B, let $\widehat\theta=(\widehat\beta_s,\widehat\beta_c)'$ and let $C$ denote the covariance of bootstrap estimates $\widehat\theta_b^*$. Define $q_{0.95}$ as the 95th percentile of
\begin{equation}
 Q_b=(\widehat\theta_b^*-\widehat\theta)'C^{-1}(\widehat\theta_b^*-\widehat\theta).
\end{equation}
The approximate joint 95\% coefficient region is
\begin{equation}
 \{\theta:(\widehat\theta-\theta)'C^{-1}(\widehat\theta-\theta)\leq q_{0.95}\}.
\end{equation}
Calibration is centered on the generator, retaining finite-sample bias in $Q_b$. Projecting this region gives phase and rate-ratio ranges and a simultaneous curve envelope. The region excludes the zero-effect pair; a region containing zero would leave phase unidentified. Coverage remains a plug-in bootstrap approximation under the fitted process \citep{davison1997bootstrap}.

C reports central 95\% raw-refit ranges and pointwise curve percentiles. These include estimator bias and are neither calibrated combined confidence intervals nor posterior intervals. B and C need not nest because their constructions differ. Angles are unwrapped around the nominal $328.1^\circ$ peak; values above $360^\circ$ continue into the next cycle. Main Figure~3 and the main Results report the effect ranges. No corresponding full-model precision analysis was performed for Barker.

\section*{Text S6. Sensitivity to analysis choices}

Exponential-history decay times of 1, 1.5, 2, 3 and 5 kyr are compared on the same exact anchors, 53 response events and 165.058-kyr exposure. $G$ spans 0.243--0.252 bits/event and preferred phase $318.2$--$330.3^\circ$; all nominal p values are below 0.00014. Separately, pre-anchor weighted history $h_{\rm pre}=0$, 0.5 or 1 is assigned to both segments at $\tau=1.5$ kyr. It contributes $h_{\rm pre}\exp(-u/\tau)$ in addition to observed history, without discarding response time. The resulting $G$ range is 0.248--0.250 and phase range $327.8$--$328.1^\circ$ (Figure~\ref{fig:S09}). These levels illustrate initial-history sensitivity rather than a calibrated uncertainty distribution.

Background alternatives add LR04 squared, \ce{CO2} squared, both squares, or both squares and their product, retaining first-order terms. Each specification enters both models, with only phase added in full. $G$ spans 0.239--0.252 bits/event and preferred phase $318.0$--$328.1^\circ$. Adding LR04 squared lowers full-model AIC by 4.77 and shifts the phase maximum to $318.2^\circ$ (Figure~\ref{fig:S10}a--c).

History alternatives replace the exponential sum with the count of strictly earlier events in the previous 1.5 kyr, elapsed time $w$ since the nearest strictly earlier event, or $\log(1+w/1\ {\rm kyr})$. The exponential and count coefficients are nonpositive; elapsed-time coefficients are unrestricted. These are changes in history representation on identical support, with history rebuilt at exact event and integration times. $G$ is 0.248, 0.219, 0.202 and 0.209 bits/event, respectively, with phase maxima $328.1$--$332.4^\circ$ (Figure~\ref{fig:S10}d--f). Adding segment-by-phase terms to the common-phase model gives nominal $p=0.810$, without establishing identical segment responses.

Barker's fixed-threshold catalogue gives $G$ 0.107 bits/event, nominal $p=0.0137$, phase $347.1^\circ$ and rate ratio 3.19 (Main Figure~2). Its own conditioning anchor changes response support (Text~S1). This is a point-age definition sensitivity; bootstrap, chronology and other sensitivity analyses use the variable-threshold catalogue.

The So-57 overlap alternative (Text~S2) retains 9,982 supported fits from 10,000 source realizations. Median primary $G$ is 0.224 bits/event (central 95\% range 0.126--0.290), and preferred phase is $328.5^\circ$ ($311.3$--$342.7^\circ$). Nominal $p<0.05$ in 9,962/9,982 valid fits (99.80\%). These results closely resemble the So-4-based main ensemble.

Separate 2,000-draw NGRIP knot-spacing checks at 2.5, 5 and 10 kyr retain 1,993, 1,997 and 1,997 supported fits, respectively. Median $G$ is 0.223, 0.223 and 0.227 bits/event, and nominal $p<0.05$ in 99.75--99.95\% of supported fits. Paired speleothem draws are fixed across spacings. These checks assess the working chronology dependence and do not inherit the main bootstrap calibration.

\section*{Text S7. Orbital alternatives and LR04 modulation}

Eight models per catalogue combine background (BG), phase (Pre), and each of eccentricity, obliquity or 65$^\circ$N summer-solstice insolation, with and without Pre. BG contains climate and history terms plus the primary segment term. Scalar orbital predictors use nominal exposure-time mean/range scaling. Daily mean top-of-atmosphere insolation uses solar longitude $90^\circ$ and solar constant 1,365 W m$^{-2}$; its native 1-kyr series is interpolated to actual event and integration times \citep{laskar2004long}.

For each scalar, we test its addition to BG, its addition after Pre, and Pre added after the scalar. A scalar adds one coefficient; Pre adds two. The nine additional comparisons form a Holm family within each catalogue, excluding the original phase test \citep{holm1979simple}. Main Figure~4 and the main Results report these comparisons. Eccentricity is additive here; an eccentricity-by-phase amplitude interaction is not tested.

Orbital and LR04-interaction analyses reuse 500 selected chronology draws per catalogue: 499 primary and all 500 Barker draws are supported. Models within each draw share events, forcing and exposure. Their age ranges do not replace null calibration.

The LR04-modulation test adds $L(u)\sin\phi(u)$ and $L(u)\cos\phi(u)$, retaining main effects. At LR04 value $L$, phase coefficients become $\beta_s+\gamma_sL$ and $\beta_c+\gamma_cL$. Figure~\ref{fig:climate_phase_comparison} evaluates this continuous model at exposure-weighted LR04 quartiles, rather than fitting separate climate groups. Added $G$ is 0.046 bits/event for the primary catalogue and 0.053 for Barker, with nominal $p=0.187$ and 0.0775. These tests provide no clear evidence of modulation; separated curves or positive nested-model $G$ alone do not establish it. Chronology dependencies and the limits of causal interpretation are discussed in the main text.

\section*{Text S8. History contribution, model fit and additional event loss}

The history test removes $H$ from the full model while retaining background climate and phase. Its null is $\beta_H=0$, with alternative $\beta_H\leq0$ at fixed $\tau=1.5$ kyr. Because the null lies on a parameter boundary, the usual $\chi^2_1$ reference need not apply \citep{self1987asymptotic}. We therefore calibrate the likelihood ratio with 4,999 no-history simulations and refits per catalogue. The observed history likelihood ratios are 8.107 (primary) and 7.722 (Barker); plus-one p values are 0.026 and 0.012, respectively. This comparison supports a history contribution within the chosen inhibitory family; it does not identify a particular physical recovery mechanism.

For full-model fit, we rescale completed inter-event intervals within each segment \citep{brown2002rescaling}:
\begin{equation}
 z_{ri}=\int_{u_{r,i-1}}^{u_{ri}}\widehat\lambda_r(u)\,du,\qquad U_{ri}=1-\exp(-z_{ri}),\qquad u_{r0}=0.
\end{equation}
Two prespecified discrepancies measure the Kolmogorov--Smirnov distance of pooled $U$ values from uniformity and adjacent dependence,
\begin{equation}
 D=\sup_{0\leq x\leq1}|\widehat F_U(x)-x|,\qquad
 T_{\rm dep}=\frac{\left|\sum_r\sum_{i=1}^{N_r-1}(U_{ri}-0.5)(U_{r,i+1}-0.5)\right|}{\sqrt{\max(1,\sum_r\max(N_r-1,0))}}.
\end{equation}
Pairs never cross record gaps. Final event-free tails contribute censored endpoint integrals but not completed intervals. Zero-response simulations have both discrepancies zero and remain in the calibration sample. We simulate the nominal full model, refit it, and recompute both statistics, thereby reproducing parameter estimation and observation stopping. This follows the simulate--refit strategy discussed by \citet{kling2026goodness}; calibration for our inhibitory process is an approximate parametric bootstrap, without invoking their Poisson-specific theoretical guarantee. The primary diagnostic reuses the 5,000 nominal full-model simulations of Text~S5; Barker uses 1,999 full-model simulations. Chronology generators are excluded. All replicates contribute. Holm correction covers these two diagnostics within each catalogue: adjusted p values are 0.167 and 0.162 for the primary catalogue and 0.623 for both Barker statistics. Neither check reveals clear misfit, but the two discrepancies cannot verify the full model or every form of temporal dependence.

Additional event loss is assessed by independently deleting each noninitial event with probability 0.1 or 0.2, retaining every original conditioning event and response boundary. We generate 500 masks per rate, applied either to both primary segments or to MIS6 alone, and separately to Barker varying-threshold events. Both models are refitted with history rebuilt from the retained events and forcing scales fixed. All refits succeed, with no nested bootstrap or chronology perturbation. At 20\% deletion from both primary segments, the central 95\% scenario range of phase shifts from the original estimate is $-14.7^\circ$ to $17.3^\circ$; for Barker it is $-19.2^\circ$ to $25.3^\circ$. These describe sensitivity to further random loss, not sampling confidence intervals or reconstruction of missing events. They cannot exclude climate- or phase-dependent detection bias.
